\section{The question}

Models now decide consumer installment credit and residential mortgage
origination. Both are regulated --- ECOA and Regulation B govern both, the Fair
Housing Act adds to mortgage. \textbf{What must a team actually do to keep bias
out of a model in these sectors, how much of that does the standard fairness
toolchain do for them, and can a language model safely do the rest?}

\section{Why it matters}

Four decisions determine whether a lending audit is \emph{legally} meaningful:
which outcome counts as favorable, who counts as protected, how much data
supports each subgroup estimate, and against whom the comparison runs. Get any
one wrong and the audit still runs, still emits a number, and still reads clean
--- the direction nobody re-examines. A false alarm costs a reviewer an
afternoon; a false clearance closes the question and the applicant never learns
why.

\section{What we did}

A complete audit of both sectors on real data: 1{,}000 consumer credit and
10{,}978 Georgia mortgage applications, every applicant scored out-of-fold by a
model that never saw them. The architecture separates two jobs.

\textbf{Deterministic layer.} Python owns every number: AIF360 computes the
metrics, a fixed rule assigns severity. This is the ground truth.

\textbf{LLM layer.} The model owns only interpretation --- who is affected, what
mechanism, what remedy. It never recomputes a metric, and any number in its
output absent from the given context is recorded as fabrication. Calculator, not
oracle.

\textbf{Benchmark.} Two tracks, kept separate. \emph{Track Q}: four prompt
strategies per attribute, severity scored against the deterministic label.
\emph{Track H}: narrative audits judged only on a checklist and rubric, never
against the numbers. 98 calls, \$0.89, cumulative cost cap, offline replay
without an API key.

\section{How it is relevant}

\textbf{The toolchain leaves the legal questions open.} Verified directly against
AIF360 0.6 and Fairlearn 0.13: AIF360 defaults \texttt{favorable\_label=1.0}, so
on a target named \texttt{loan\_default} it treats defaulting as the good
outcome; Fairlearn exposes no favorable-label parameter at all. Neither library
has \emph{any} concept of minimum subgroup size --- no floor, no warning, no
guidance. Neither binds a protected class to a statute.

\textbf{Intersectional cells stay unidentified even at full population.} Six of
twelve HMDA race$\times$sex cells have no determinate severity band:
\texttt{American Indian $\times$ Male} is $0.6734$ with a $95\%$ interval of
$[0.45, 0.90]$ at $n{=}31$ --- consistent with a critical violation and with
passing. Growing the audit fivefold did not resolve them. The defensible
disparities are the large cells: Black women and men at $0.863$/$0.864$, intervals
wholly below parity, which a point-estimate ranking demotes to sixth and seventh.

\textbf{The statute matters more than the metric.} Auditing age at $40{+}$
(ADEA, employment law) returns $0.9791$ --- clean. At $62{+}$, the cut ECOA
specifies, the same model returns $\mathbf{0.8897}$: the largest age disparity
present.

\textbf{An LLM is a usable interpreter, not a usable measurer.} Aggregate
agreement of $75\%$ decomposes to $33\%/92\%/100\%$ and tracks task difficulty
rather than capability. Grounding the prompt in retrieved literature cut
fabricated citations $16 \rightarrow 2$, a larger effect than any prompt
strategy. Prompt sensitivity is generation-specific: spread across four
strategies is $14.7$ points on \texttt{gpt-4o} and $\mathbf{0.0}$ on
\texttt{gpt-5-mini}.

\section{How it applies}

\textbf{1.} State the favorable outcome explicitly; never accept the default.
\textbf{2.} Audit the book, not a test split --- in mortgage, HMDA already
requires filing it. \textbf{3.} Put the statute citation beside the threshold in
code: ECOA $62{+}$, not ADEA $40{+}$; note that \S1002.6(b)(2) permits
\emph{favouring} elderly applicants, so a ratio above 1 is lawful and a symmetric
metric cannot express that. \textbf{4.} Publish what was suppressed, with $n$ and
the unreported ratio. \textbf{5.} Record the reference group beside every ratio.
\textbf{6.} Let the model interpret, never compute, and score it against numbers
it cannot move.
