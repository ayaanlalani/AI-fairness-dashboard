# Stage 1 Findings — Deterministic Lending-Lifecycle Baseline

Answers to the drill-down questions in docs/RESEARCH_STAGING_PROMPT.md §2,
grounded in `lending_lifecycle_baseline.md` (generated by
`scripts/intersectional_drilldown.py`) and the per-dataset fairness artifacts.
Deterministic pipeline only — no LLM was used for any number or finding here.

## UC1 — Consumer installment-credit scoring (German Credit)

- **Q1.1 (age × sex compounding): YES, strongly.** Marginal DI is borderline
  for both sex (0.8595) and age (0.8212), but the intersection is far worse:
  `under_40 × female` has a selection rate of 0.6087 vs 1.0000 for
  `40_plus × female` — an intersectional DI of **0.6087**, well below the 0.72
  CRITICAL threshold that neither marginal attribute reaches. Age and sex
  disadvantage compound; the joint subgroup (n=46, adequately sized) is the
  most disadvantaged group in this use case.
- **Q1.2 (foreign-worker disparity): data-scarcity artifact at the group level.**
  Only n=6 test rows are non-foreign-workers, below the n≥15 reporting floor.
  The marginal DI of 0.9402 is dominated by the n=194 foreign-worker group;
  any claim about the small group is unstable. Test-set evidence cannot
  distinguish a real national-origin-proxy effect from noise — flagged as a
  limitation, not a finding.
- **Q1.3 (age proxies):** see `artifacts/german_credit/fairness/qualitative_report.md`
  (proxy-detection section) — correlation-based proxy screening is produced by
  the deterministic qualitative engine per attribute.

## UC2 — Mortgage underwriting (HMDA Georgia)

- **Q2.1 (race × sex intersectional denial gaps): joint disparity exceeds both
  marginals.** Marginal DI: race 0.8956, sex 0.9291 (both borderline-moderate).
  Intersectionally, `Black × Female` (n=410) sits at DI **0.7821** against
  `Asian × Male` (n=130) — a 17.6-point selection-rate gap. The intersectional
  view moves the audit conclusion from "borderline" to "HIGH-range" severity
  for a large, adequately-sized subgroup.
- **Q2.2 (within-strata persistence):** not yet computed — requires joining
  income/loan-amount strata into the predictions CSV; queued as a Stage 2
  candidate drill-down.
- **Q2.3 (metric disagreement):** race shows DI = 0.8956 failing the 0.95
  borderline threshold while EqualOpportunityDiff (0.0025) and
  AverageOddsDiff (0.0117) are near zero: selection *rates* differ by race,
  but error rates conditional on the true label barely do. For an underwriting
  audit this pattern points at base-rate differences / historical label bias
  rather than differential model error — exactly the case where demographic
  parity and equalized-odds framings disagree.

## UC3 — P2P personal-loan default risk (Lending Club)

- **Q3.1 (gender DI = 0.0): confirmed as a metric-orientation artifact, not a
  real disparity.** The dataset-local `compute_fairness.py` computes rates on
  the *default* (label 1) prediction: male default-prediction rate is 0.0000
  vs female 0.0069, so DI = 0/0.0069 = 0.0 and severity reads CRITICAL. When
  oriented on the favorable outcome (predicted non-default), selection rates
  are 1.0000 (male) vs 0.9931 (female) — DI **0.9931**, i.e. near-parity.
  **Action item:** align `lending_club_dataset/scripts/compute_fairness.py`
  favorable-outcome orientation with the shared audit spec before any Track Q
  run on this use case; until then the normalized CSV's gender row must carry
  this caveat wherever it is quoted.
- **Q3.2 (income × loan-size compounding): no meaningful disparity in
  predictions.** All intersectional selection rates are ≥ 0.9848. Root cause:
  the classifier predicts non-default for ~99.5% of the test set (only 4 of
  600 rows get a default prediction) — a near-constant classifier on a highly
  imbalanced target. Fairness metrics are therefore nearly degenerate for this
  model; the honest audit finding is *insufficient model discrimination to
  measure disparity*, not "fair".
- **Q3.3 (economic proxies vs legal classes):** the contrast with UC1/UC2 is
  that UC3's near-degenerate predictions make every group look equal — a
  reminder that fairness metrics are only informative when the model actually
  separates classes. This becomes a limitation paragraph for the paper and a
  quality gate for Track Q (dataset must have non-trivial prediction variance).

## Cross-cutting

1. **Intersectional analysis changes conclusions in both credit scoring and
   mortgage underwriting** (UC1: 0.61 vs 0.82 marginal; UC2: 0.78 vs 0.90
   marginal). Marginal-only audits understate severity — this is the paper's
   strongest deterministic result and the human-centered hook (single-axis
   audits miss the people at the intersections).
2. **Small-subgroup floors matter everywhere:** foreign workers (n=6), four
   HMDA race groups (n≤10), one Lending Club cell (n=9) fall below n=15 and
   are reported-but-not-interpreted.
3. **Semantic Scholar availability:** the public (keyless) API was
   rate-limited (HTTP 429) throughout this run, so the regenerated
   `qualitative_report.md` files carry empty inline research sections for this
   cycle. The evidence JSONs (`qualitative_research_evidence.json`) for German
   Credit and HMDA are retained from the prior successful harvest (3 papers
   per attribute) — they contain paper lists only, no metric values, so no
   inconsistency is introduced. Re-run the qualitative step off-peak (or with
   `SEMANTIC_SCHOLAR_API_KEY`) to refresh the inline citations.
4. **AverageOddsDiff sign convention:** this run flipped the sign of AOD for
   every attribute in German Credit and HMDA relative to the previously
   committed CSVs (magnitudes identical). The two compute paths
   (AIF360 vs. manual fallback) disagree on orientation
   (privileged−unprivileged vs. the reverse). Pin one convention in each
   dataset's `compute_fairness.py` before Track Q, since severity narratives
   quote the sign.
5. **Environment note:** the previously reported `make fairness` hang did not
   reproduce under Homebrew `python3.11`; it is attributable to the repo's
   `.venv`/`venv` native libraries mmap-timing-out (errno=60) under iCloud
   Drive eviction (repo lives under `Desktop/`). Reviewers should use a local,
   non-synced interpreter — the Makefile already prefers `python3.11`.
